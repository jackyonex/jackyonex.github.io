A.3 Warp the images

warpImageNearestNeighbor and warpImageBilinear both use inverse warping. I send the four corners of the input through H to get the bounding box of the output, then send every output pixel back through the inverse of H to the input. Nearest neighbor rounds to the nearest pixel, and bilinear takes the weighted average of the four neighboring pixels. An alpha mask marks the output pixels that fall inside the input, and the rest stay black.

For rectification I photographed a poster on a laundromat wall from its left side and from its right side. In each photo I clicked the four poster corners and mapped them to a rectangle. The whole photo is warped.

Zoomed in, nearest neighbor gives jagged letters and brick edges, and bilinear gives smooth but slightly softer edges.

A.4 Blend the images into a mosaic

I keep the right photo unwarped as the reference and warp the left photo into it. I find the mosaic size from the warped corners and warp both photos into it in one shot. Each photo gets an alpha that is 1 at its center and falls linearly to 0 at its edges. I warp the alpha with the photo, and each mosaic pixel is the sum of alpha times color divided by the sum of the alphas. I tried each photo as the reference and kept the right one, where the building and the cars keep their shape better.

Part B: Feature Matching for Autostitching

Part B uses the three gym photos.

B.1 Harris corners and ANMS

I found the corners with the provided harris.py and applied adaptive non-maximal suppression from Section 3 of the paper. Each corner gets a radius, the distance to the nearest corner that is clearly stronger (its own strength is below 0.9 times the other one), and I keep the 500 corners with the largest radius.

B.2 Feature descriptors

I take a 40 × 40 window around each corner from a Gaussian blurred copy of the photo and sample every fifth pixel to get an 8 × 8 patch, then subtract the mean and divide by the standard deviation. I tried σ = 1, 2 and 3 on the matches in B.3. With σ = 1 fewer points match, and with σ = 3 more long wrong lines cross the photos, so I kept σ = 2.

B.3 Feature matching

For each descriptor I find the closest and second closest descriptor in the other photo and keep the match when the ratio of their squared distances is below a threshold. In Figure 6b of the paper the incorrect matches stay low below a ratio of about 0.6 and pass the correct matches at about 0.67, so I tried thresholds of 0.5, 0.6 and 0.7. With 0.5 fewer points match, and with 0.7 more long wrong lines cross the photos, so I kept 0.6.

B.4 RANSAC and the mosaic

Each RANSAC round picks 4 matches at random, computes H with computeH from Part A, and counts the matches that land within δ pixels after mapping. I keep the largest set of inliers and compute H again from all of them by least squares. I tried δ = 1, 2 and 3 pixels on the mosaic. With δ = 1 the floor line shows twice, and with δ = 2 and 3 it is a single line, so I kept δ = 2.

The mosaic uses the Part A code with the middle photo as the reference. For the manual mosaic I clicked 8 points for each pair. The manual and automatic mosaics look almost the same, and the hoop and the floor lines line up in both.
